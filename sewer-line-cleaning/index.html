Main line backup: what to do first

The main line takes everything leaving the house. When it is blocked, the lowest drain is the first to overflow, and that is often a shower or tub on the ground floor.

What to do while you wait

Stop all water use right away. That means no flushing, no laundry and no dishwasher.

Move belongings from the lowest floor drain. Keep pets and children out of the area, and wash hands after any contact.

Find the cleanout outside, often a capped pipe near the house. Tell the plumber where it is.

Why main lines clog

The main line is the least forgiving drain in a house. Tree roots enter through joints, paper and wipes catch on rough spots, grease cools and coats the pipe, and a sag called a belly holds water and debris.

If a clear lasts only weeks, the camera usually shows why. That conversation leads to sewer inspection and, if needed, repair.

Cleanouts: the access that makes this fast

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line. With one, the plumber can put a machine straight into the line. Without one, the machine goes in through a toilet or a roof vent, which takes longer and carries more risk of damage.

If your house lacks a cleanout, installing one makes every future clearing cheaper and safer. We tell you whether yours is in the right place.

What is different about cleaning a main line

Main line machines use larger cables and heavier cutters than the machines used for sinks. They reach farther and cut roots out of a four-inch pipe. Hydro jetting can follow to scour the wall.

After the line is clear, we run a camera. That shows whether the blockage was a one-time event or the sign of a defect. Skipping the camera is how the same backup returns a month later.

For lines with heavy grease, see hydro jetting. For the smaller drains that feed the main line, see drain cleaning.

Preventive cleaning and maintenance

A line with roots or grease does not fix itself. A schedule matched to the cause avoids midnight backups.

  • Root-prone lines: cut roots on a regular schedule until the entry point is repaired.
  • Grease-prone lines: jet on a schedule and fix the habits that put grease down the sink.
  • Older homes: camera the line before buying or after repeated backups.
  • Rental properties: a clear and a camera at turnover reduces surprises.

Septic systems and sewer connections

Some properties around Florence are on septic systems. A blocked line to a septic tank has different causes, and a full tank is a pumping job, not a drain clearing job. Tell us at the start if you are on septic so we plan the right visit and tell you plainly whether it is within what we handle.
