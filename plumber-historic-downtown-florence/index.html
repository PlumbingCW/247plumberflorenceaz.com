About Historic Downtown Florence

The Town of Florence says Levi Ruggles founded the Florence Townsite in 1866. McFarland State Historic Park, at 24 West Ruggles Street, holds the first Pinal County Courthouse, built in 1878. The Silver King Marketplace at 440 N Main Street began as the Silver King Hotel in 1895.

A regional magazine describes more than 100 homes and commercial properties in the district on the National Register, in styles from Sonoran to American Victorian and Mission Revival. Homes.com adds that the town core includes homes built in the 1800s.

This page is about the district around Main Street. For the rest of the Town and nearby communities, see the Florence service area.

Sources: Town of Florence walking tour page, Golden Corridor Living magazine, Homes.com neighborhood guide and Wikipedia.

What older buildings mean for plumbing

Age alone does not tell us what is behind a wall. Some buildings have been updated, and others have not. We do not assume any particular pipe material. What we do say is that a camera and pressure check take away guesswork before a repair opens a wall or a floor.

What to do while you wait on a plumbing emergency

These steps limit damage in an older building.

  • Close the main water valve if a pipe is leaking or burst. See burst pipe repair.
  • Stop using drains if sewage is backing up. See sewer line cleaning.
  • Turn off power to the water heater if the tank is leaking. See water heater repair.
  • Photograph damage and note the time for your insurance claim.

Water, permits and historic rules downtown

The Town of Florence Utilities Department provides water and sewer service for the Town, and its office is at 425 E Ruggles Street, 520-868-7060. Our service area page names Florence Water as the Town system and notes that its 2025 report lists no hardness figure. Ask the Town for the latest report, and see our water softener and filtration page before buying a softener.

Permits inside town limits go through the Community Development Department at 224 W. 20th Street, 520-868-7575. Plumbing work follows the code the Town has adopted, usually based on the UPC or IPC. Historic properties may also have review rules for visible exterior work, so ask Community Development whether your address is inside the district before planning anything beyond the plumbing itself.

Sources: Town of Florence Utilities Department page and Community Development contact information.

The annual home tour and keeping a historic home sound

The Town hosts a historic home tour every February. The 39th tour opened 15 historic properties, according to the Town. If you own a historic home, a camera look, a pressure check and a water heater inspection are sensible items for your maintenance list.
