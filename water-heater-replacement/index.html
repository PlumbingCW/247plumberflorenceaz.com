When a failed water heater is an emergency

A leaking tank that is spreading water through a garage or closet is an emergency. A heater that only runs cold can usually wait for the next available visit.

What to do while you wait

Close the cold water supply valve at the top of the heater. If it will not turn, close the main water valve.

Switch off the gas control or the heater breaker so no one forgets it is still powered. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Signs the heater is past repair

Some repairs are not worth making. These signs usually point to replacement.

  • Water leaking from the bottom or side of the tank shell.
  • Rust-colored hot water that returns after flushing, a sign of tank corrosion.
  • A unit that has kept running past a decade and now needs a major part.
  • Repeated repairs within a short time.
  • A noisy, rumbling tank that overheats because of heavy sediment.

Choosing the replacement: gas, electric, hybrid or tankless

The right replacement depends on your fuel, your space and how much hot water the house uses.

What happens on replacement day

A typical replacement is a single visit, though gas conversions or venting corrections can add time.

  1. 1Shut off and drain.We close the supply, cut power or gas and drain the old tank.
  2. 2Remove and prepare.The old unit goes out, and we check the shutoffs, flex lines and pan.
  3. 3Set and connect.The new heater is set level, with new flex connectors, a relief valve and discharge line.
  4. 4Test.We fill, purge air, test for leaks, light or power the unit and check venting.
  5. 5Explain.You learn the thermostat setting, the shutoffs and a flushing schedule.

If you are choosing a heater before the old one fails, read water heater installation.

Permits, licensing and the tax credit

Permit rules vary by jurisdiction, so we recommend asking the building department for your address. The Town of Florence Community Development Department is at 224 W. 20th Street and can be reached at 520-868-7575, though its public page does not describe plumbing permit rules. Pinal County Building Safety is at 85 N Florence Street for unincorporated areas.

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, according to the IRS, applied to improvements made through December 31, 2025. That means a heat pump water heater installed in 2026 no longer qualifies. Check the IRS site and your utility for current programs before relying on any credit.

To verify any plumber, look up the license with the Arizona Registrar of Contractors at roc.az.gov. Plumbing appears there under classifications such as C-37, CR-37 and R-37.

Sources: Town of Florence, Pinal County, IRS, Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

What replacement costs and what moves the price

You approve a price before we start. These are the factors.

Fuel and type

Tankless and heat pump units cost more than a standard tank.

Tank size

Larger tanks and higher recovery ratings add cost.

Venting and gas line

A new vent or a longer gas line adds labor and materials.

Code and safety items

Expansion tank, pan, drain line and straps where the local code calls for them.

Removal and access

Attic and tight-closet installs take longer.

Homeowner policies generally cover resulting water damage from a sudden failure but not the cost of the heater. Ask your agent.

Code note: permits and inspection

A water heater swap is often a permitted job. The UPC and IPC set requirements for relief valves, drain pans in some locations and venting. Ask your building department which code edition and which permit rules apply to your address.
