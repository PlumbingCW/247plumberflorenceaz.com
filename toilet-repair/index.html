When a toilet problem is an emergency

Most toilet trouble can wait for the next visit. These cannot.

  • Water or waste spilling over the rim onto the floor.
  • A steady leak from the base, the tank bolts or the supply line that you cannot stop.
  • A toilet that keeps filling and will not shut off, with the shutoff valve stuck open.
  • Several fixtures backing up at once, which points to the main line.

What to do while you wait

Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet. If it will not close, shut the main water valve at the house.

Stop flushing. Mop up and keep people away from any waste. If several fixtures are affected, treat it as a main line problem and stop all water use.

Running, weak and phantom flushes

Most toilet complaints are about the parts inside the tank. A flapper that no longer seals lets water leak into the bowl, and the fill valve then turns on every few minutes. That is a phantom flush, and it wastes a lot of water.

A weak flush usually means mineral scale in the rim jets, a partly blocked trap or a low water level in the tank. A tank that fills slowly, hisses or overflows into the overflow tube points to a worn fill valve.

Find a silent toilet leak with food coloring

A toilet can leak into the bowl without making a sound. The EPA says the test is simple. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait about ten minutes without flushing and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

The EPA also notes that fixing leaks that are easy to find can save about ten percent on a water bill. If the test shows a leak, replacing a flapper or fill valve is one of the least expensive plumbing fixes there is.

Source: EPA WaterSense, Fix a Leak Week.

Leaks at the base and rocking bowls

Water on the floor around a toilet can come from three places: the supply line, the tank-to-bowl bolts or the seal at the base. A failing wax ring or a cracked flange allows water to seep past the seal each time the toilet is flushed.

Where the water is coming from
Where it shows upLikely causeTypical fix
Puddle at the base after flushingFailed wax ring or loose bowlReset the toilet with a new seal.
Drips under the tankWorn tank gasket or boltsReplace gasket and hardware.
Wet shutoff valve or supply lineCorroded valve or loose connectionReplace the valve or line.
Rocking bowl with damp floorBroken or loose flangeRepair or replace the flange.

Water that soaks into the subfloor can rot it. A toilet that rocks or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, even without an obvious puddle.

Clogged toilets that will not plunge out

A toilet clog is usually in the trap built into the bowl. A closet auger reaches it. If the auger clears the toilet and a second fixture still backs up, the blockage is further down the line. That moves the job to drain cleaning or to sewer line cleaning.

Never use a wire hanger or a chemical cleaner in a toilet. Both can damage the porcelain or hurt you.

Installing a new toilet and repairing the flange

If the old toilet is cracked, constantly clogs or uses far more water than it should, a new one makes sense. Installation includes setting a new wax or foam seal, tightening the bolts evenly, connecting a new supply line and testing the flush.

  • Check the rough-in distance from the wall to the center of the drain before buying a toilet.
  • Choose between round and elongated bowls and standard or comfort height.
  • A broken flange needs a repair ring or replacement before the toilet goes back on.
  • Subfloor damage, if found, should be repaired before the new toilet is set.

Code note: flush volume and clearances

Plumbing codes set maximum flush volumes and minimum clearances around toilets. A replacement should meet both, which is one reason to choose a model with the EPA WaterSense label.
