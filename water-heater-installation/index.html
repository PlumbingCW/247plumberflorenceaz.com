Sizing a water heater for your household

Undersized heaters run out in the middle of a shower. Oversized ones waste money and floor space. Tank capacity matters, but so does the first-hour rating, which describes how much hot water the heater can deliver in an hour from a full tank.

  • One to two people with a single bathroom often do well with a smaller tank.
  • A family with several showers and a laundry day needs a larger tank or a higher recovery rating.
  • A house with a soaking tub or a large kitchen needs more hot water capacity than the headcount suggests.

We ask how many bathrooms you have, what runs at the same time and what the old unit did well or poorly.

Gas, electric, hybrid or tankless

Each type suits a different setup. Gas heaters recover quickly and need venting and a gas supply. Electric tanks are simpler to install and need the right breaker. Heat pump heaters use less electricity but need space and airflow. Tankless heaters save floor space and supply hot water on demand, but they need adequate gas and exhaust.

Installation details that get missed

A good install is mostly in the details around the heater.

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a properly routed discharge line.
  • New flexible connectors and shutoff valves so the heater can be isolated later.
  • An expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system.
  • A drain pan and drain line where a leak could damage finished space.
  • Correct venting for gas heaters, with draft tested after install.

New construction, additions and remodels

In new construction the heater location is often set in the plans, and we coordinate with the builder on supply, drain and venting. For remodels and additions, the question is usually whether the existing gas line, vent and panel can support the heater you want.

In newer subdivisions such as Anthem at Merrill Ranch, ask what the builder installed before you choose, because the vent path and the gas line size decide what you can add. Older homes in the Townsite area may need new supply lines or a different vent before they can take a new type of heater.

Permits and who to ask

Permit requirements vary, so ask the building department that covers your address. In town that is the Florence Community Development Department at 224 W. 20th Street, 520-868-7575. Pinal County Building Safety at 85 N Florence Street handles unincorporated areas, and Pinal County also handles permits for San Tan Valley under its agreement with the Town.

We install to the plumbing code the jurisdiction has adopted and can tell you what to expect for inspection. To check any contractor, use the license lookup at the Arizona Registrar of Contractors.

Code note: placement and clearances

The UPC and IPC set rules for combustion air, venting, seismic or strap support where adopted and relief valve discharge. A new location for a heater needs more review than a swap, and we walk through those requirements with you.
