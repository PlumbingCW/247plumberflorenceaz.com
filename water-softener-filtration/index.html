Find out whether your water is hard

Do not buy a softener before you know your number. The Town of Florence water quality page and the 2025 Consumer Confidence Report for Florence Water do not list hardness, so testing your tap is the reliable first step. Hardness varies by utility and by well.

  1. 1Ask your supplier.Florence Water serves the Town, EPCOR serves the Anthem area and parts of San Tan Valley, and Arizona Water Company serves Coolidge.
  2. 2Read the water quality report.Look for hardness in grains per gallon or milligrams per liter.
  3. 3Test your tap.A home test kit or a lab test tells you what arrives at your sink.
  4. 4Compare with the scale.The USGS classes water as soft up to 60 mg/L, moderately hard from 61 to 120, hard from 121 to 180 and very hard above 180.

Sources: Town of Florence, EPCOR, Arizona Water Company, USGS.

What EPCOR reports for its San Tan system

EPCOR's 2025 water quality report for its San Tan system lists hardness of 15 to 18 grains per gallon from groundwater. One grain per gallon equals 17.1 mg/L, so that range is about 257 to 308 mg/L, well above the USGS threshold for very hard water.

The report covers the San Tan system and does not name every neighborhood, so check your own bill or ask EPCOR which system serves your address before assuming that number applies to you.

What hard water does to plumbing and appliances

Dissolved calcium and magnesium leave scale on heating elements, inside water heaters and on faucet aerators. Scale insulates the bottom of a tank heater from the burner and clogs a tankless heat exchanger. It spots glass, stiffens laundry and leaves film on shower doors.

A softener removes the hardness before it reaches the pipes. It does not filter contaminants, and it adds sodium in exchange. The 2025 Florence Water report lists sodium of 110 ppm, and the Arizona Water Company Pinal Valley report lists 170 to 290 ppm. People on sodium-restricted diets should ask a doctor about softened drinking water.

Repairing a softener that stopped working

Most softener complaints trace to a few causes: a salt bridge that stops brine from forming, a stuck control valve, a failed resin bed, a clogged injector or a power or timer fault. We check the settings, test the water before and after the unit and repair or replace the failed part.

If the system is old and parts are scarce, we tell you whether repair or replacement makes sense. Water conditioner repair and valve replacement are part of this work.

Installing a system

A proper installation includes a bypass valve, a drain connection for regeneration and an outlet for power. Placement matters because the unit needs a drain and a power source. We route it ahead of the water heater so scale never reaches it.

Tell us if you have a tankless heater. Softened water protects the heat exchanger, and we can set it up with isolation valves for later flushing. See tankless water heater for details.

Code note: drains and backflow at treatment systems

Softeners discharge regeneration water, and the UPC and IPC require an air gap or other approved connection at that drain. Treatment equipment should also meet the standards its maker lists, so ask for the model's certification.
