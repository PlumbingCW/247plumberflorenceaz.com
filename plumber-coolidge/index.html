A town built on irrigated farmland

Coolidge was founded in 1925 and incorporated in 1945. The Coolidge Dam, finished in 1930, brought reliable irrigation and made cotton the local crop. The 2020 census counted 13,218 residents.

That farm history matters under the ground. Many older Coolidge lots sit on flat, irrigated soil with mature trees, and long sewer laterals that run to the street. Roots find the joints in old clay and cast iron pipe, which is why repeat main line clogs are common in the older blocks.

If a drain keeps backing up after you clear it, a camera is the fastest way to see why. See sewer camera inspection and sewer line cleaning.

Water in Coolidge: what the utility report says

Arizona Water Company serves Coolidge. Its office is at 351 N Arizona Blvd, and the phone is (520) 723-5346. The 2025 Pinal Valley report that covers the Casa Grande and Coolidge areas lists groundwater as the source and sodium between 170 and 290 ppm. It does not list a hardness figure.

That means the honest first step on scale is to test your tap or ask the utility for its hardness number. A heater or fixture that scales quickly is the signal to check. Treatment options are on our water softener and filtration page.
