What counts as a plumbing emergency

Some problems cannot wait, and others can. Knowing the difference saves money and stress.

  • Water flowing from a broken pipe or fitting that you cannot stop.
  • Sewage backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • A leaking or failed water heater, or no hot water in cold weather.
  • A hot spot on the floor or a sudden rise in your water bill.
  • Any gas smell. Leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

What to do while you wait

Close the main water valve, switch off the water heater and move belongings off the floor.

Take photos for your insurance claim and keep people and pets away from any wastewater.

Why homeowners call before the damage spreads

Water does not stay where it starts. A leak behind a wall soaks insulation and drywall, and a sewer backup spreads across tile and carpet. Calling early limits the damage and the repair bill.

Plumbing work is governed by the code the local building department has adopted, usually based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) or the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Water quality and your plumbing

Where your water comes from affects how long fixtures last. Florence Water uses four groundwater wells, and its 2025 report lists no hardness figure. EPCOR's San Tan system lists 15 to 18 grains per gallon, which is very hard. Test your tap before you buy a softener.
