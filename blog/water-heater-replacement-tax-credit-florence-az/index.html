Water Heater Replacement Tax Credit: A Florence, AZ Update

Many homeowners search for a tax credit before buying a water heater. For installs this year, the federal credit most people remember is no longer available, and it is better to know that before you plan around it.

This guide explains what the credit covered, when it ended and where to look for other help. It is general information, not tax advice.

Call (480) 741-9332

By the Florence 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

What the credit was

The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit, found in Section 25C of the tax code, allowed a credit for qualifying home energy improvements. For heat pumps and heat pump water heaters, the annual cap was $2,000.

The credit applied to improvements made through December 31, 2025. That means a unit installed in 2026 does not qualify under that section.

What this means for a 2026 replacement

Do not count on the credit when you set a budget.

  • A quote that assumes a federal credit for a 2026 install is out of date.
  • A heater installed and placed in service in 2025 may have qualified, so check your 2025 tax return with a preparer.
  • Rules can change, so check the IRS site for the current year.
  • A tax professional can say how your own situation is treated.

Compare operating cost, not only price

Without a credit, the decision rests on purchase price, installation and running cost. A more efficient unit can cost more up front and less each month. A heat pump water heater uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank, but it needs space and airflow.

If you are choosing between a tank and a tankless unit, see tankless water heater for how the two differ. For a straight swap of a failed tank, water heater replacement covers the steps.

Types of heaters and their running cost

Without a credit, the running cost often decides which heater wins.

A heat pump unit is worth a look in a garage or large utility room with room to breathe.

Keeping records for any future incentive

Programs come and go, and new ones may appear. A tidy file lets you claim anything that applies.

  • The invoice showing the model, the price and the date.
  • The manufacturer's data sheet or efficiency rating.
  • Photos of the installed unit and its label.
  • The permit and inspection record, if there was one.
  • The warranty card or registration confirmation.

Store them together, on paper and as photos. Tax preparers and utilities usually ask for the same few items.

How to treat any claim you read online

Many pages about energy credits are out of date. A page that does not state its date or cite the IRS should be treated with care. Rules change, and a quote that depends on a credit must be checked against the official source.

Look for the tax year and the end date. If a page says a credit is available but does not say for which year, assume nothing. When in doubt, ask a tax professional.

What to do if you installed in 2025

If your water heater was installed and placed in service in 2025, ask your preparer whether it qualified. Gather the invoice and the product specifications. The preparer will tell you which form applies.

If you are planning a 2026 project, budget as if there is no federal credit. Treat any rebate you find as a bonus and not as part of the plan.

Local note: where the rule comes from

The credit is a federal rule, not an Arizona or Pinal County program. The primary source is the IRS, which publishes the rules for the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit and notes the December 31, 2025 end date for property placed in service.

We are plumbers, not tax advisors. We do not know your tax situation, and nothing here replaces advice from a preparer. Keep your invoice from any 2025 installation in case a preparer asks for it.
