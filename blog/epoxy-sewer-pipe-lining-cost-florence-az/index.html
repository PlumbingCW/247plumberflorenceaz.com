Epoxy Sewer Pipe Lining Cost in Florence, AZ: What to Know

Epoxy lining repairs a damaged sewer pipe from the inside, so most of the yard stays untouched. It can save a lot of disruption, but it is not right for every pipe.

This guide covers what lining is, which factors decide the price and the conditions where it will not work.

By the Florence 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

What lining is

A resin-soaked liner is pulled into the old pipe and inflated. The resin hardens and forms a new pipe inside the old one. It seals cracks, gaps at joints and root entry points, and it leaves a smooth surface.

The liner reduces the pipe diameter slightly, but the smooth surface usually carries flow as well as the old rough pipe did. Lining is one form of trenchless repair, which means little or no trenching.

What decides the price

Lining is priced by scope, not by a flat rate. These factors move the number.

Cost factors for pipe lining

Length of the runMore liner and more timeLonger costs more.

Because the cost depends on a camera inspection, the first step is usually a sewer camera inspection.

When lining works well

Lining suits pipes that are cracked, leaking at joints or invaded by roots but still hold their basic shape.

  • Long, straight runs under driveways, patios or landscaping.
  • Pipe with root entry at joints.
  • Cast iron or clay with cracks but no collapse.
  • Lines where digging would be expensive or disruptive.

When lining will not work

A liner needs a pipe that is open and roughly round. Some pipes cannot be lined.

  • A collapsed pipe that the liner cannot pass.
  • A severe belly that holds standing water, because the liner follows the sag.
  • A badly offset joint that blocks the liner.
  • A pipe with a very small diameter or many tight bends.

In those cases, excavation or pipe bursting is the right fix. See sewer line repair for the options.

Before you sign

Ask to see the camera footage that justifies lining. Ask what happens to branch connections and how the plumber will reopen them. Ask for the warranty in writing and for the permit plan. A clear answer to each question is a sign of a well-planned job.

How the lining process unfolds

A lining job follows a defined sequence, and each stage affects the cost.

  1. 1Inspect.A camera records the pipe and finds the damage.
  2. 2Clean.Hydro jetting or cutting removes roots, scale and grease.
  3. 3Install the liner.A resin-soaked liner is pulled into place and inflated.
  4. 4Cure.The resin hardens with heat, steam or light.
  5. 5Reinstate and verify.Branch openings are cut open and a final camera run confirms the result.

Cleaning is not optional. A liner will not bond to a coated wall.

Questions about longevity and resale

Buyers and lenders sometimes ask for records on sewer work. Keep the camera footage from before and after, the warranty and any permit paperwork with your house file.

A lined pipe is not invisible to an inspector. Many accept it as a documented repair. A written record answers questions quickly and may help a sale. Ask the installer what documents they provide at the end of the job.

Comparing quotes fairly

Two quotes for lining can differ by a wide margin for good reasons. One may include cleaning, reinstatement of branches, permits and a final camera run. Another may leave out several of those.

  • Ask for the length of liner in feet.
  • Ask for the liner thickness and material.
  • Ask whether cleaning and reinstatement are included.
  • Ask whether the quote covers permit fees.
  • Ask for the warranty term and what it covers.

What a camera shows before lining starts

A before-and-after camera record is your proof of the work. The first pass shows cracks, roots and sags. The second shows a smooth, continuous liner and clean branch openings.

If the camera shows a collapse partway down the run, a liner cannot pass it. Better to learn that before the crew mobilizes. That is why the camera visit comes first and the quote comes second.

Local note: older lines near the Florence Townsite

The Florence Townsite Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on October 26, 1982, and includes more than 25 buildings. Lines serving older properties near the center of town may be older materials, which is exactly where a camera inspection pays off before any lining decision.
