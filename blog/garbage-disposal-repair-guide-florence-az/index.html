Garbage Disposal Repair: A Florence, AZ Homeowner Guide

A garbage disposal that hums but will not spin, trips the breaker or leaks under the sink is a common kitchen problem. Many cases clear in minutes with a reset or a hex key. Others need a new unit.

This guide walks through the safe checks in order, explains what each symptom points to and tells you when to stop and call.

Safety first: never put a hand in the disposal

Before any troubleshooting, cut the power. Switching off the wall switch is not enough, because someone may flip it back on.

  • Unplug the disposal under the sink, or turn off its breaker at the panel.
  • Never reach in by hand. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object.
  • Use a flashlight to look into the opening before you touch anything.
  • Keep children away from the kitchen until the unit is safe again.

The sound a disposal makes points to the cause.

Free a jammed disposal

Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the bottom. Power must be off before you use it.

  1. 1Insert the hex key.Fit the wrench into the socket at the bottom of the unit.
  2. 2Work it back and forth.Turn until the flywheel moves freely in both directions.
  3. 3Remove the cause.Look inside with a flashlight and pull out any bone, utensil or bottle cap with tongs.
  4. 4Press reset.Push the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  5. 5Restore power and test.Run cold water, switch the disposal on briefly and listen.

If the unit trips again right away, the motor is probably failing. Continued resets can overheat it.

Leaks under the sink

A wet cabinet floor sends many people straight to a new disposal, but the leak may be somewhere cheaper to fix. Dry the area, run water and watch where it appears first.

Water at the top rim points to the sink flange. Water at the side points to the drain elbow or the dishwasher hose. Water from the bottom of the body means the housing has failed and the unit must be replaced.

Leaks that are ignored swell the cabinet floor and can feed mold. If you cannot find the source, our leak detection service can trace it.

Habits that keep a disposal running

A disposal is a small grinder, not a trash can. These habits extend its life.

  • Run cold water before, during and after each use.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts rather than a full load.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds in bulk, bones, pasta and rice out of the unit.
  • Skip fibrous items such as celery, corn husks and potato peels.
  • Grind a handful of ice now and then to knock loose residue.

Grease is the main cause of kitchen drain clogs further down the line. See drain cleaning when the sink backs up after you fix the disposal.

When replacement is the better choice

A disposal lasts several years, and the cost of a repair adds up. Replace it when the housing leaks, the motor keeps tripping or the unit is rusty and loud. Pick a motor size to match how you cook, and make sure the mounting fits your sink.

Choosing a replacement disposal

If a replacement is the answer, a few specs matter more than the brand name. Match them to how you cook and to the space under your sink.

Tell the installer if you have a septic system, since some units suit septic tanks better than others.

Smells, slow draining and the dishwasher connection

A disposal that smells is usually dirty and not broken. Grind a few ice cubes with a halved lemon, run cold water and wipe the rubber splash guard with a brush. If the smell returns, food is trapped in the drain line past the disposal.

The dishwasher drain hose connects to a small port on the side of the disposal. A new disposal has a knockout plug in that port. Forgetting to remove the plug is a classic mistake, and it makes the dishwasher back up into the sink. If the dishwasher will not drain after an install, check that plug first.

Replacing a disposal is usually a minor job, but rules differ by jurisdiction.
