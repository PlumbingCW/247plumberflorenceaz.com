How to Plumb a Toilet: A Florence, AZ Homeowner Guide

Replacing a toilet is one of the more approachable plumbing jobs. A new toilet connects to two things, a water supply and a drain, and the steps are the same every time.

This guide covers the parts, the order of work and the mistakes that cause leaks. It also covers when a job belongs with a licensed plumber.

The parts you are connecting

A toilet looks simple, but it has several parts that must seal.

Toilet connection parts
PartJobCommon problem
Closet flangeAnchors the toilet to the floor and meets the drainCracked or set too low.
Wax ring or gasketSeals the toilet to the flangeLeaks if reused or misaligned.
Closet boltsHold the toilet to the flangeOvertightened bolts crack the bowl.
Supply lineBrings water from the shutoff to the tankOld lines burst.
Shutoff valveStops the water for serviceStuck or leaking valve.

Gather tools and check the flange first

Collect an adjustable wrench, a putty knife, a new wax ring or foam gasket, new closet bolts, a new supply line, a level, rags and a bucket. Then check the flange. The flange should sit flat on the finished floor and not be cracked or corroded.

A flange set below the floor surface leaves a gap that a standard wax ring cannot fill. A spacer ring or a repair flange fixes this. If the flange is broken or the floor is soft, stop, because the problem is larger than the toilet.

Remove the old toilet

Take the old one out cleanly before you set the new one.

  1. 1Shut off the water.Close the valve and flush to empty the tank.
  2. 2Disconnect the supply.Unscrew the line and sponge out the remaining water.
  3. 3Remove the nuts.Take the caps off the closet bolts and remove the nuts.
  4. 4Lift the toilet.Rock it gently to break the seal and lift with a helper.
  5. 5Clean the flange.Scrape off old wax and stuff a rag in the drain to block sewer gas.

Set the new toilet

Place new closet bolts in the flange slots, set the new ring and lower the toilet straight down so it seats evenly.

  1. 1Seat the bowl.Press down firmly with a slight twist and do not rock it.
  2. 2Tighten the nuts.Alternate sides in small turns until snug. Overtightening cracks porcelain.
  3. 3Attach the tank.Follow the maker's steps for tank bolts and gaskets.
  4. 4Connect the supply.Use a new line and tighten by hand plus a quarter turn.
  5. 5Test.Open the valve, flush several times and check every joint for moisture.

Caulk around the base if you like, but leave a gap at the back so a leak can show.

Mistakes that cause leaks

Most failures trace to a few errors. Reusing an old wax ring, rocking the toilet after it seats, tightening one bolt fully before the other and using an old supply line are the usual ones. A toilet that rocks needs shims, not more tightening.

If a toilet leaks at the base after a swap, the seal is bad. Pull it and reset it with a new ring. Water that appears days later on the ceiling below is a sign the seal failed. Our toilet repair page covers running, clogged and leaking toilets.

When to hire the job out

Call a plumber if the flange is broken, the floor is soft, the drain is slow or you are adding a new toilet where none existed. New drain and vent routing is real plumbing work. See plumbing installation for new bathrooms and remodels.

Choosing the right toilet for your bathroom

The best toilet is one that fits the space and the drain. Measure before you shop.

Buy a model with a rough-in that matches your wall. A 10-inch toilet will not fit a 12-inch flange without compromise.

Measurements to take
MeasureHowWhy
Rough-inWall to center of the flange boltsMost are 12 inches, but check.
Bowl shapeRound or elongatedElongated needs more room.
HeightStandard or comfort heightComfort height suits many adults.
ClearanceSpace to side walls and in frontCodes set minimums.

Water use and efficiency labels

Newer toilets use much less water per flush than older ones. The EPA WaterSense label marks models that meet its water efficiency criteria. A toilet with that label can lower your water bill, especially in a house with several people.

Look at the flush rating on the tank or the box. Check that the bowl performance has been tested. If a toilet needs two flushes to clear the bowl, it saves nothing. Read independent flush tests before buying a low-cost model.

After the install: a quick checklist

Do a final check an hour later and again the next day. Look at the base for moisture, press on the tank to see whether it moves and listen for a hiss that means the fill valve is still running.

Mark the shutoff valve with a tag so everyone in the house knows where it is. Keep the old supply line out of the trash until you are sure the new connection holds.

Local note: licensing in Arizona

Arizona licenses contractors through the Registrar of Contractors. Plumbing classifications include C-37, CR-37, R-37 and R-37R. You can check a license at roc.az.gov or by calling 602.542.1525 before you hire anyone to do more than a simple swap.

For a like-for-like toilet swap by the owner, ask your building department whether a permit is needed. In Florence that is Community Development at 520-868-7575, and in unincorporated areas it is Pinal County Building Safety.
