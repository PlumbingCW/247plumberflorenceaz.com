When a slab leak is an emergency

A slab leak wastes water every minute and can undermine the soil beneath the floor. These signs mean act now.

  • Water coming up through the floor or along the baseboards.
  • A hot spot on tile or concrete that is not near the water heater, which can mean a hot water line leak.
  • A hissing or trickling sound in the floor when everything is off.
  • A sudden spike in the water bill or a meter that moves with all fixtures off.

What to do while you wait

Close the main water valve and switch off the water heater breaker or gas control so the heater does not run dry.

Move rugs and furniture, keep electronics off wet flooring and photograph the damage for your records.

How slab leaks start

Water lines buried in or under the slab can fail for several reasons. Pipe in constant contact with concrete and soil can corrode or abrade over the years. Movement of the slab can stress joints. High water pressure can add strain.

Pinal County has a particular ground concern. The Arizona Geological Survey explains that heavy groundwater pumping can cause land subsidence and earth fissures, and fissures are documented in Pinal County. Not every slab leak has anything to do with ground movement, but if you see an open crack in the ground near your home, keep people away and report it to the survey and your local emergency management office.

We do not diagnose ground movement. We find and repair the water line, and tell you if the evidence points to something a geotechnical specialist should look at.

Source: Arizona Geological Survey, earth fissures and subsidence.

How we locate the leak without breaking the floor first

Finding the exact spot is the most valuable step because it keeps the repair small.

  1. 1Isolate the line.We pressure-test hot and cold lines separately to see which one is leaking.
  2. 2Listen.Acoustic devices pick up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe.
  3. 3Scan for heat.Thermal imaging shows warm patches from a hot water leak.
  4. 4Mark.We mark the floor and the pipe path so the repair is a small, accurate opening.

Water damage, flooring and your insurance

A slab leak can soak flooring, baseboards and drywall before it is found. We stop the leak and repair the pipe, but drying out and rebuilding flooring is a restoration job.

Policies differ on slab leaks. Many cover resulting water damage but not the pipe itself or the cost of finding the leak. Document everything and ask your agent before work begins.

Code note: testing and permits

The UPC and IPC call for testing repaired water piping before it is concealed. Slab work often needs a permit, so ask the building department that covers your address, and expect an inspection of the repair or reroute.
