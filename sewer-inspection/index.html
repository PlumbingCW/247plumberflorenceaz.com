What a camera inspection tells you

A camera answers the question a cable cannot: what is actually wrong. It also tells you what is not wrong, which can save you from an unneeded repair.

What the camera can show
FindingWhat it meansUsual next step
Roots at jointsRoots are entering through gapsCut roots, then repair the joint.
Offset or separated jointPipe sections have shiftedSpot repair or lining depending on severity.
Belly (sag)Water and debris pool in a low spotMonitor, clean regularly or replace the section.
Cracks or fracturesPipe wall is damagedLining or spot repair.
Scale or grease buildupPipe bore is shrinkingHydro jetting.
CollapsePipe has failedExcavation or trenchless replacement.

What to do with each finding is spelled out on our sewer line repair page.

When to book an inspection

The best time is before a problem becomes an emergency.

  • A drain that clogs repeatedly despite being cleared.
  • Before buying a home, especially an older one.
  • After a backup, to find the cause.
  • Before lining or any major repair, to plan the work.
  • When trees are growing near the sewer line.
  • When a landlord wants to confirm the condition of a rental.

Buying a home in Florence: why the sewer line is worth checking

A general home inspection does not normally put a camera in the sewer line. A seller may not know the line has roots or a belly. A camera visit before closing gives you the footage to negotiate or to plan the repair.

Homes in the Florence Townsite Historic District date back well over a century in some cases, so the line there may be an older material. Newer subdivisions such as Anthem at Merrill Ranch have newer pipe, but construction debris and installation faults still show up on camera.

How the inspection works

The visit is short and clean when there is a cleanout.

  1. 1Access.We open the cleanout, or pull a toilet if there is none.
  2. 2Run the camera.A flexible rod carries the camera from the house toward the street, recording footage and depth.
  3. 3Locate.A surface locator tracks the camera so we can mark depth and position of any defect.
  4. 4Review.You see the video and hear what each finding means.
  5. 5Plan.If repair is needed, we outline the options. If not, we tell you the line is sound.

If the line needs clearing before the camera can pass, we do that first. See sewer line cleaning and drain cleaning.

What an inspection cannot show

A camera shows the inside of the pipe, not the soil around it. It cannot predict future root intrusion, and a recent clearing can hide a defect temporarily. A line that is full of water or debris may not be viewable until it is cleaned.

If the camera cannot pass a collapse or a severe offset, the footage up to that point still helps plan the excavation.

Septic systems and sewer laterals

Tell us whether your home connects to a sewer or a septic tank. A camera can inspect the line leading to a septic tank, but the tank and leach field are a different inspection. We tell you which part we can see and which part needs a septic specialist.

Code note: what an inspection does not replace

A camera visit is not a code inspection. Building departments apply the plumbing code adopted locally, usually based on the UPC or IPC. Our footage shows condition, and the department decides what a permit requires.
